How Paycheck Taxes Work in the United States

Understanding the American tax system is crucial for financial planning. When you receive a paycheck, several layers of taxation are applied before funds hit your bank account. The main components are federal income tax, state income tax (in most states), and FICA taxes for Social Security and Medicare.

The Federal Income Tax System

The United States uses a progressive tax system for federal income taxes, meaning higher income levels are taxed at higher rates. For 2026, we expect seven tax brackets ranging from 10% to 37%, though exact brackets will be adjusted for inflation. The standard deduction for single filers is projected to be approximately $14,600, and $29,200 for married couples filing jointly.

State Income Taxes Vary Widely

State taxation adds another layer of complexity. Nine states have no income tax at all (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming), while others like California and New York have top rates exceeding 10%. Some states use a flat tax rate, while others have progressive systems similar to the federal government.

FICA: Social Security and Medicare

FICA taxes are separate from income taxes and fund specific social programs. Social Security tax is 6.2% on income up to the wage base limit ($168,600 in 2024, adjusted annually). Medicare tax is 1.45% on all earnings, with an additional 0.9% surtax on high-income earners (over $200,000 for singles, $250,000 for married couples).

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this paycheck tax calculator?

Our calculator provides accurate estimates based on current 2026 tax projections, standard deductions, and average state tax rates. For exact calculations accounting for all deductions and credits, consult a tax professional.

What taxes are deducted from my paycheck?

Typically, federal income tax, state income tax (in most states), Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and sometimes local taxes. Additional deductions may include health insurance, retirement contributions, and other benefits.

Why is my take-home pay different from my salary?

Your salary is gross income before taxes and deductions. Take-home pay (net income) is what remains after all mandatory taxes and voluntary deductions are subtracted.

What is the difference between marginal and effective tax rate?

Your marginal tax rate is the rate on your last dollar of income (your highest bracket). Your effective tax rate is the average rate you pay on all your income (total tax ÷ total income).

How do I change my tax withholdings?

Submit a new Form W-4 to your employer. The form was redesigned in 2020 to be more accurate. Use our calculator to determine the right withholding amount before submitting.

Are bonuses taxed differently?

Yes, bonuses are typically taxed at a flat 22% federal rate for supplemental wages up to $1 million. State taxes also apply. However, when you file your annual return, bonuses are included with regular income.

What happens if I have too much tax withheld?

You'll receive a refund when you file your tax return. While some people enjoy large refunds, this means you've given the government an interest-free loan. Consider adjusting your W-4 to have more accurate withholding.

Do I pay Social Security tax on all my income?

No. For 2024, Social Security tax applies only to the first $168,600 of earnings. This limit adjusts annually for inflation. Medicare tax, however, applies to all earnings with no cap.

How do self-employment taxes work?

Self-employed individuals pay both the employee and employer portions of FICA taxes (15.3% total), plus income taxes. They can deduct the employer-equivalent portion when calculating adjusted gross income.

What tax changes are expected for 2026?

Unless extended, several provisions from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act expire after 2025. This may mean higher tax rates, lower standard deductions, and changes to many deductions and credits.
